The rules
  • Ground state: the electron is in the lowest level it can be in.
  • Excitation: the electron absorbs energy (heat, electricity or light) and jumps to a higher level. This excited state is unstable.
  • De-excitation: the electron falls to a lower level and emits one photon. Photon energy = Ehigh − Elow.
  • Bigger drop → more energy → higher frequency → shorter wavelength (toward violet). A smaller drop gives a longer wavelength (toward red).
  • Dropping in steps emits one photon per step. 4 → 2 and then 2 → 1 gives two photons.
  • For these six levels: drops to n = 2 are visible, drops to n = 1 are ultraviolet, and drops to n = 3, 4 or 5 are infrared.
  • Only certain levels exist, so only certain colors are possible. That is why you get lines and not a rainbow.

The matching rule
  1. An element is present only if all of its lines appear in the unknown.
  2. One shared line is not enough. Sodium and neon both have a line at 616 nm.
  3. Lines left over belong to another element.

Each element has its own set of energy levels, so its line spectrum is a fingerprint that no other element has.

Hydrogen wavelengths are worked out from 1/λ = R(1/nlow² − 1/nhigh²). The other elements use rounded classroom values.
Line colors on screen are close to the real ones, but not exact.
