2. Polynomials, identities and the two theorems

Degree = highest power of the variable. Linear (1), quadratic (2), cubic (3).

Identities (memorize):

(a+b)2=a2+2ab+b2(a−b)2=a2−2ab+b2a2−b2=(a+b)(a−b)(a+b)^2 = a^2 + 2ab + b^2 \qquad (a-b)^2 = a^2 - 2ab + b^2 \qquad a^2 - b^2 = (a+b)(a-b)

(x+a)(x+b)=x2+(a+b)x+ab(x+a)(x+b) = x^2 + (a+b)x + ab

a3+b3=(a+b)(a2−ab+b2)a3−b3=(a−b)(a2+ab+b2)a^3 + b^3 = (a+b)(a^2 - ab + b^2) \qquad a^3 - b^3 = (a-b)(a^2 + ab + b^2)

Remainder Theorem. When a polynomial p(x)p(x) is divided by (x−a)(x - a), the remainder is p(a)p(a).

Factor Theorem. (x−a)(x - a) is a factor of p(x)p(x) exactly when p(a)=0p(a) = 0.

Example. Is (x−2)(x-2) a factor of p(x)=x3−3x2+4p(x) = x^3 - 3x^2 + 4? p(2)=8−12+4=0p(2) = 8 - 12 + 4 = 0, so yes.

3. Factoring — pick the method in this order

  1. Common factor first: 6x2+9x=3x(2x+3)6x^2 + 9x = 3x(2x + 3).
  2. Two terms: difference of squares a2−b2=(a+b)(a−b)a^2 - b^2 = (a+b)(a-b). Example: 4x2−25=(2x+5)(2x−5)4x^2 - 25 = (2x+5)(2x-5). A sum of squares a2+b2a^2 + b^2 does not factor over the real numbers.
  3. Three terms ax2+bx+cax^2 + bx + c: split the middle term. Find two numbers with product acac and sum bb, rewrite bxbx with them, then factor by grouping.
  4. Four terms: group in pairs.

Why splitting works: (px+q)(rx+s)=prx2+(ps+qr)x+qs(px + q)(rx + s) = pr\,x^2 + (ps + qr)x + qs. The two middle pieces psps and qrqr add to bb and multiply to (pr)(qs)=ac(pr)(qs) = ac.

Example (a=1a = 1). x2+7x+12x^2 + 7x + 12: product 12, sum 7 → 3 and 4. Answer (x+3)(x+4)(x+3)(x+4).

Example (a≠1a \neq 1). 6x2+11x−106x^2 + 11x - 10: product −60-60, sum 1111 → 1515 and −4-4.

6x2+15x−4x−10=3x(2x+5)−2(2x+5)=(3x−2)(2x+5)6x^2 + 15x - 4x - 10 = 3x(2x + 5) - 2(2x + 5) = (3x - 2)(2x + 5)

Sign guide: c>0c > 0 → both numbers have the sign of bb. c<0c < 0 → opposite signs; the larger one has the sign of bb.

Always check by multiplying back.

4. Quadratic equations (new)

Standard form: ax2+bx+c=0ax^2 + bx + c = 0, with a≠0a \neq 0. The solutions are also called roots or zeros; on a graph they are the x-intercepts.

4.1 Four ways to solve

(1) Factoring + Zero Product Property. If A⋅B=0A \cdot B = 0 then A=0A = 0 or B=0B = 0. One side must be 0 first.

x2−5x=14⇒x2−5x−14=0⇒(x−7)(x+2)=0⇒x=7 or x=−2x^2 - 5x = 14 \;\Rightarrow\; x^2 - 5x - 14 = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; (x - 7)(x + 2) = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; x = 7 \text{ or } x = -2

(2) Square roots (when there is no xx term). Remember the ±\pm.

3x2−48=0⇒x2=16⇒x=±4(x−1)2=9⇒x−1=±3⇒x=4 or −23x^2 - 48 = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; x^2 = 16 \;\Rightarrow\; x = \pm 4 \qquad\qquad (x - 1)^2 = 9 \;\Rightarrow\; x - 1 = \pm 3 \;\Rightarrow\; x = 4 \text{ or } -2

(3) Completing the square. Add (b2)2\left(\frac{b}{2}\right)^2 to both sides (when a=1a = 1).

x2+6x−7=0⇒x2+6x=7⇒x2+6x+9=16⇒(x+3)2=16⇒x=−3±4x^2 + 6x - 7 = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; x^2 + 6x = 7 \;\Rightarrow\; x^2 + 6x + 9 = 16 \;\Rightarrow\; (x + 3)^2 = 16 \;\Rightarrow\; x = -3 \pm 4

So x=1x = 1 or x=−7x = -7.

(4) Quadratic formula. Works for every quadratic.

x=−b±b2−4ac2ax = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}

4.2 Derivation of the quadratic formula (completing the square on the general equation)

ax2+bx+c=0ax^2 + bx + c = 0

Divide by aa and move the constant:

x2+bax=−cax^2 + \frac{b}{a}x = -\frac{c}{a}

Add (b2a)2\left(\frac{b}{2a}\right)^2 to both sides:

x2+bax+b24a2=b24a2−ca⇒(x+b2a)2=b2−4ac4a2x^2 + \frac{b}{a}x + \frac{b^2}{4a^2} = \frac{b^2}{4a^2} - \frac{c}{a} \;\Rightarrow\; \left(x + \frac{b}{2a}\right)^2 = \frac{b^2 - 4ac}{4a^2}

Take square roots and solve for xx:

x+b2a=±b2−4ac2a⇒x=−b±b2−4ac2ax + \frac{b}{2a} = \pm\frac{\sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a} \;\Rightarrow\; x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}

Example. 2x2−3x−5=02x^2 - 3x - 5 = 0: a=2a = 2, b=−3b = -3, c=−5c = -5.

b2−4ac=9+40=49x=3±74⇒x=52 or x=−1b^2 - 4ac = 9 + 40 = 49 \qquad x = \frac{3 \pm 7}{4} \;\Rightarrow\; x = \frac{5}{2} \text{ or } x = -1

4.3 The discriminant D=b2−4acD = b^2 - 4ac

Discriminant Roots Graph

Example. x2+4x+5=0x^2 + 4x + 5 = 0: D=16−20=−4<0D = 16 - 20 = -4 < 0, no real roots. With i=−1i = \sqrt{-1} (so i2=−1i^2 = -1): x=−4±2i2=−2±ix = \frac{-4 \pm 2i}{2} = -2 \pm i.

4.4 Sum and product of the roots

If the roots are r1r_1 and r2r_2:

r1+r2=−bar1r2=car_1 + r_2 = -\frac{b}{a} \qquad r_1 r_2 = \frac{c}{a}

Why: expand a(x−r1)(x−r2)a(x - r_1)(x - r_2) to get ax2−a(r1+r2)x+ar1r2ax^2 - a(r_1 + r_2)x + a\,r_1 r_2, then match the coefficients with ax2+bx+cax^2 + bx + c.

A quadratic with roots r1,r2r_1, r_2: x2−(sum)x+(product)=0x^2 - (\text{sum})x + (\text{product}) = 0. Roots 3 and −5-5: x2+2x−15=0x^2 + 2x - 15 = 0.

Three forms:

  • Standard: y=ax2+bx+cy = ax^2 + bx + c (shows the y-intercept cc)
  • Vertex: y=a(x−h)2+ky = a(x - h)^2 + k (vertex (h,k)(h, k))

Example. y=x2−4x−5y = x^2 - 4x - 5. Axis x=42=2x = \frac{4}{2} = 2. Vertex y=4−8−5=−9y = 4 - 8 - 5 = -9, so vertex (2,−9)(2, -9), a minimum. Vertex form y=(x−2)2−9y = (x - 2)^2 - 9. Factored y=(x−5)(x+1)y = (x - 5)(x + 1), x-intercepts 55 and −1-1. y-intercept (0,−5)(0, -5).

4.6 Word problem pattern (US units)

Height of an object thrown upward: h(t)=−16t2+v0t+h0h(t) = -16t^2 + v_0 t + h_0 (feet, seconds).

Example. A ball is thrown up at 48 ft/s from a height of 64 ft: h=−16t2+48t+64h = -16t^2 + 48t + 64.

  • Maximum height: t=−482(−16)=1.5t = -\frac{48}{2(-16)} = 1.5 s; h=−16(2.25)+72+64=100h = -16(2.25) + 72 + 64 = 100 ft.
  • Hits the ground: −16t2+48t+64=0-16t^2 + 48t + 64 = 0; divide by −16-16: t2−3t−4=0t^2 - 3t - 4 = 0; factor: (t−4)(t+1)=0(t - 4)(t + 1) = 0; so t=4t = 4 s (reject t=−1t = -1).

5. Statistics

5.1 Collecting data

Term Meaning
Population / sample the whole group / the part actually measured
Parameter / statistic a number describing a population (μ\mu, σ\sigma) / describing a sample (x‾\bar{x})
Bias a method that systematically favors some outcomes

Sampling methods: simple random (every member equally likely) · systematic (every kkth) · stratified (random sample from every group) · cluster (all members of a few random groups) · convenience and self-selected (both biased).

Ways to collect data: survey · observational study (watch, no treatment; shows association only) · experiment (treatment is assigned at random; the only method that can show cause) · simulation.

Bias to look for: undercoverage, non-response, leading question wording, self-selection.

5.2 Center

  • Mean: x‾=∑xn\bar{x} = \frac{\sum x}{n}. Grouped data: x‾=∑fixi∑fi\bar{x} = \frac{\sum f_i x_i}{\sum f_i}, where xix_i is the class mark (midpoint).
  • Median: middle value of the ordered data (average of the two middle values when nn is even). Not pulled by outliers.
  • Mode: most frequent value.
  • Grouped median: l+N2−cff×hl + \frac{\frac{N}{2} - cf}{f} \times h · Grouped mode: l+f1−f02f1−f0−f2×hl + \frac{f_1 - f_0}{2f_1 - f_0 - f_2} \times h (ll = lower limit of the class, hh = class width, cfcf = cumulative frequency before the median class, f1f_1 = modal frequency, f0f_0 and f2f_2 = the frequencies before and after).
  • Skewed data: report the median. Symmetric data: mean = median.

5.3 Spread

  • Range = max − min.
  • Standard deviation: σ=∑(x−x‾)2n\sigma = \sqrt{\frac{\sum (x - \bar{x})^2}{n}} ; variance =σ2= \sigma^2.

Check. Scores 6, 7, 7, 8, 8, 8, 8, 9, 9, 10: mean 8; squared distances add to 12; σ=1.2≈1.10\sigma = \sqrt{1.2} \approx 1.10.

5.4 Graphs

Bar graph: categories, gaps between bars. Histogram: continuous classes, no gaps; with unequal class widths plot adjusted frequency =frequencyclass width×smallest width= \frac{\text{frequency}}{\text{class width}} \times \text{smallest width}. Frequency polygon: join the class marks.

5.5 Normal distribution

Bell-shaped, symmetric about the mean.

  • Empirical rule: 68% within μ±σ\mu \pm \sigma · 95% within μ±2σ\mu \pm 2\sigma · 99.7% within μ±3σ\mu \pm 3\sigma.
  • z-score: z=x−μσz = \frac{x - \mu}{\sigma} ; back to a value: x=μ+zσx = \mu + z\sigma.
  • The table gives the area to the left of zz. Right: 1−1 - table. Between: table(bb) − table(aa).

Example. μ=70\mu = 70, σ=5\sigma = 5. What percent is between 65 and 80? 65 is −1σ-1\sigma, 80 is +2σ+2\sigma: 34%+34%+13.5%=81.5%34\% + 34\% + 13.5\% = 81.5\%. And x=82x = 82 gives z=82−705=2.4z = \frac{82 - 70}{5} = 2.4.

5.6 Margin of error and hypothesis testing

  • Margin of error for a sample proportion: ±1n\pm\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}. For n=400n = 400: ±0.05=±5%\pm 0.05 = \pm 5\%. A poll result of 52% means the true value is likely between 47% and 57%. To halve the margin of error, use 4 times the sample size.
  • Hypothesis test. Null hypothesis = the claim being tested. Significance level α\alpha = the cutoff (usually 5%).

Rule: if the observed result (or one more extreme) shows up in α\alpha or less of the simulated samples, reject the null hypothesis. Otherwise do not reject (this does not prove the claim).

Example. 36 heads in 50 flips appeared in 0 of 200 fair-coin simulations (0% ≤ 5%): reject “the coin is fair”. 29 heads appeared in 39 of 200 (19.5% > 5%): do not reject.
