Session M4 — Exponential Functions: Growth, Decay and Their Graphs

Duration: 45 minutes
Continues from: Session M1 (laws of exponents, rational exponents, same-base exponential equations, hidden quadratics) and the midterm review M3. Midterm score 94/100, so the pace is brisk and Tier 4 is a real challenge.
School unit: Unit 2, “Exponential and Logarithmic Functions” (Georgia standard AA.FGR.3: inverse functions, exponential and logarithmic graphs, logarithm properties, modeling). This session is the first of the unit.
Foundation: R.D. Sharma Exponents of Real Numbers (laws of exponents), already covered in M1.
School textbook: Big Ideas Advanced Algebra with CalcChat and CalcView (Larson & Boswell), chapter “Exponential and Logarithmic Functions”: the lessons on exponential growth and decay functions, and the natural base e.
Why now: Unit 2 starts when school resumes. Every later idea in the unit (inverse functions, logarithms, solving exponential equations) is built on the functions introduced today.

Lesson Plan

Objectives. By the end of this session the student should be able to:

  1. Tell a linear pattern (constant difference) from an exponential one (constant ratio).
  2. Define f(x)=a⋅bxf(x) = a \cdot b^x, explain the restrictions on b, and state its key features.
  3. Write growth and decay models y=a(1+r)ty = a(1 + r)^t and y=a(1−r)ty = a(1 - r)^t, including half-life and doubling time.
  4. Derive and use the compound-interest formula, and explain where e comes from.
  5. Graph y=a⋅bx−h+ky = a \cdot b^{x - h} + k from its asymptote and anchor points.
  6. Write an exponential function through two points, and solve equations by a common base.

Part 1 — Linear vs Exponential (10 min)

Constant difference vs constant ratio

x 0 1 2 3 4
f(x)=3x+2f(x) = 3x + 2 2 5 8 11 14
g(x)=2⋅3xg(x) = 2 \cdot 3^x 2 6 18 54 162
  • f adds 3 each time x goes up by 1: a constant difference. That is a linear function.
  • g multiplies by 3 each time x goes up by 1: a constant ratio. That is an exponential function.

Test for a table with equally spaced x-values: subtract consecutive y-values (all equal → linear); divide them (all equal → exponential).

Definition

An exponential function has the form

f(x)=a⋅bx,a≠0,b>0,b≠1f(x) = a \cdot b^x, \qquad a \ne 0,\; b > 0,\; b \ne 1
a is the initial value (f(0)=a⋅b0=af(0) = a \cdot b^0 = a) and b is the base, the constant ratio (the growth or decay factor).

Why these restrictions on b:

  • b > 0: with a negative base, (−2)1/2=−2(-2)^{1/2} = \sqrt{-2} is not a real number, so the function would have gaps; and its values would flip sign at every whole number: (−2)1=−2(-2)^1 = -2, (−2)2=4(-2)^2 = 4.
  • b ≠ 1: 1x=11^x = 1 for every x, which is a constant (linear) function, not an exponential one.
  • a ≠ 0: 0⋅bx=00 \cdot b^x = 0 for every x.

Growth and decay

Key feature (for a>0a > 0) Value Reason

If a<0a < 0, the graph is reflected across the x-axis: the range becomes y<0y < 0.

Part 2 — Growth and Decay Models (13 min)

Derivation: the percent model

A quantity starts at a and grows by a fixed percent r (as a decimal) each period.

  • After 1 period: a+ar=a(1+r)a + ar = a(1 + r).
  • After 2 periods: a(1+r)⋅(1+r)=a(1+r)2a(1 + r) \cdot (1 + r) = a(1 + r)^2.
  • After t periods: multiply by (1+r)(1 + r) t times:

growth: y=a(1+r)tdecay: y=a(1−r)t\text{growth: } y = a(1 + r)^t \qquad\qquad \text{decay: } y = a(1 - r)^t

The growth factor is b=1+rb = 1 + r; the decay factor is b=1−rb = 1 - r. Read the rate back from the base: b=1.07b = 1.07 means growth of 7%; b=0.85b = 0.85 means decay of 15% (because 1−0.85=0.151 - 0.85 = 0.15).

Worked Example 1: population growth

A town of 12,000 people grows 3% per year. Write a model and predict the population after 10 years.

  • a=12000a = 12\,000, r=0.03r = 0.03, so P(t)=12000(1.03)tP(t) = 12\,000(1.03)^t.
  • P(10)=12000(1.03)10=12000×1.3439=16127P(10) = 12\,000(1.03)^{10} = 12\,000 \times 1.3439 = 16\,127 people.

Worked Example 2: depreciation

A car bought for $28,000 loses 15% of its value each year. Find its value after 5 years.

  • Decay factor b=1−0.15=0.85b = 1 - 0.15 = 0.85: V(t)=28000(0.85)tV(t) = 28\,000(0.85)^t.
  • V(5)=28000(0.85)5=28000×0.44371V(5) = 28\,000(0.85)^5 = 28\,000 \times 0.44371 = $12,423.75.

Half-life and doubling time

If a quantity halves every h time units, then after t units it has halved t/ht/h times:

y=a(12)t/hand if it doubles every d:y=a⋅2t/dy = a\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^{t/h} \qquad\qquad \text{and if it doubles every } d: \quad y = a \cdot 2^{t/d}

Worked Example 3. A 400 mg dose of a medicine has a half-life of 6 hours.

  • After 24 hours (4 half-lives): 400(12)24/6=400×116=25400\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^{24/6} = 400 \times \tfrac{1}{16} = 25 mg.
  • After 9 hours: 400(12)1.5=141400\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^{1.5} = 141 mg. The exponent doesn’t have to be a whole number.

Worked Example 4. A bacteria culture of 500 cells doubles every 20 minutes. After 2 hours (120 min) it has doubled 120/20=6120/20 = 6 times: 500⋅26=32000500 \cdot 2^6 = 32\,000 cells.

Derivation: compound interest

A principal P earns an annual rate r, compounded n times a year. Each compounding period pays a rate of r/nr/n, so the money is multiplied by (1+r/n)(1 + r/n) each period. In t years there are ntnt periods:

A=P(1+rn)ntA = P\left(1 + \frac{r}{n}\right)^{nt}

Worked Example 5. $5,000 is invested at 4% for 10 years.

Compounding n Amount after 10 years
annually 1 5000(1.04)105000(1.04)^{10} = $7,401.22
monthly 12 5000(1+0.04/12)1205000(1 + 0.04/12)^{120} = $7,454.16
daily 365 5000(1+0.04/365)36505000(1 + 0.04/365)^{3650} = $7,458.96
continuously — 5000e0.45000e^{0.4} = $7,459.12

More frequent compounding pays more, but the gain shrinks: it approaches a limit.

Where the number e comes from

Invest $1 at 100% interest for one year, compounded n times: A=(1+1n)nA = \left(1 + \frac{1}{n}\right)^n.

n (1+1n)n\left(1 + \frac{1}{n}\right)^n
1 2
2 2.25
4 2.4414
12 2.6130
365 2.7146
10,000 2.7181
1,000,000 2.7182805

The values level off at e = 2.718281828…, an irrational number like π. It is the most a dollar can grow in a year at 100% interest.

Continuous compounding. Write m=n/rm = n/r, so n=mrn = mr and rn=1m\frac{r}{n} = \frac{1}{m}:

(1+rn)nt=(1+1m)mrt=[(1+1m)m]rt\left(1 + \frac{r}{n}\right)^{nt} = \left(1 + \frac{1}{m}\right)^{mrt} = \left[\left(1 + \frac{1}{m}\right)^m\right]^{rt}

As n grows without limit, so does m, and the bracket approaches e:

A=PertA = Pe^{rt}

Part 3 — Graphs and Transformations (8 min)

y=a⋅bx−h+ky = a \cdot b^{x - h} + k

Parameter Effect on the graph of y=bxy = b^x
h shifts right by h (left if h is negative)
k shifts up by k; the asymptote moves to y = k
|a| > 1 or < 1 vertical stretch or shrink
a < 0 reflection across the asymptote (over the x-axis before the shift)

Sketching method:

  1. Draw the asymptote y=ky = k as a dashed line.
  2. Plot an anchor point at x=hx = h: there b0=1b^0 = 1, so y=a+ky = a + k.
  3. Plot one or two more points (x = h + 1 and x = h − 1, or the y-intercept).
  4. Draw the curve approaching the asymptote on one side.

Worked Example 6. Graph y=2⋅3x−1−4y = 2 \cdot 3^{x - 1} - 4.

  • Asymptote y=−4y = -4; range y>−4y > -4; domain all real numbers.
  • x=1x = 1: y=2(1)−4=−2y = 2(1) - 4 = -2. x=2x = 2: y=2(3)−4=2y = 2(3) - 4 = 2. x=0x = 0: y=2(13)−4=−103≈−3.33y = 2\left(\tfrac{1}{3}\right) - 4 = -\tfrac{10}{3} \approx -3.33 (the y-intercept).
  • The graph crosses the x-axis between x=1x = 1 and x=2x = 2, where 3x−1=23^{x-1} = 2. To find that x exactly, you need a logarithm (next session): x=1+log32≈1.63x = 1 + \log_3 2 \approx 1.63.

Part 4 — Writing from Two Points, and Solving (5 min)

Worked Example 7: two points

Find y=a⋅bxy = a \cdot b^x through (1,6)(1, 6) and (3,54)(3, 54).

  • Substitute both: 6=ab6 = ab and 54=ab354 = ab^3.
  • Divide the second by the first to cancel a: ab3ab=546\dfrac{ab^3}{ab} = \dfrac{54}{6}, so b2=9b^2 = 9 and b=3b = 3 (b must be positive).
  • Then a=6/b=2a = 6/b = 2. Answer: y=2⋅3xy = 2 \cdot 3^x. Check: 2⋅33=542 \cdot 3^3 = 54 ✓

Solving

  • Same base: 8x=328^x = 32: 23x=252^{3x} = 2^5, so x=53x = \tfrac{5}{3}.
  • Bases don’t match: when does the town in Example 1 double? Solve 1.03t=21.03^t = 2. A table gives 1.0323=1.9741.03^{23} = 1.974 and 1.0324=2.0331.03^{24} = 2.033, so it doubles between 23 and 24 years. The exact answer needs the logarithm, the inverse of an exponential function, which is next session: t=log2log1.03≈23.4t = \dfrac{\log 2}{\log 1.03} \approx 23.4 years.

Most common mistakes

  1. Using the rate instead of the factor: decay of 15% means b=0.85b = 0.85, not b=0.15b = 0.15 or b=1.15b = 1.15.
  2. Applying the coefficient before the power: 2⋅3x2 \cdot 3^x is not 6x6^x. The exponent applies only to the 3.
  3. Forgetting to divide the rate and multiply the time in compound interest: monthly at 4% for 10 years is (1+0.04/12)120(1 + 0.04/12)^{120}.
  4. Moving the asymptote the wrong way: only k moves it; h never does.
  5. Taking b=−3b = -3 from b2=9b^2 = 9: the base of an exponential function must be positive.
  6. Writing the half-life exponent upside down: it is t/ht/h (elapsed time ÷ half-life), not h/th/t.
