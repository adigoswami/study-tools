Forces & net force 8 cards
What is a force?›

A push or a pull on an object, caused by an interaction with another object. It is a vector (size + direction), measured in newtons (N).

Contact force vs field force?›

Contact forces need touching (push, normal, friction, tension, air resistance). Field forces act at a distance (gravity, magnetic, electric).

What is the "agent" of a force?›

The object that exerts it (the table, the Earth, the rope). If you can't name an agent, the force isn't real.

What is the net force?›

The vector sum of all the forces acting on one object. In 1D: choose a positive direction and add with signs.

10 N right and 4 N left act on a box. Net force?›

6 N right

Balanced vs unbalanced forces?›

Balanced: F_net = 0, so the motion doesn't change. Unbalanced: F_net ≠ 0, so the object accelerates.

What is 1 newton?›

The net force that gives a 1 kg mass an acceleration of 1 m/s². 1 N = 1 kg·m/s².

Is there a "force of motion" that keeps a thrown ball going?›

No. Nothing exerts it. The ball keeps moving because of inertia (First Law).

First Law & inertia 10 cards
State Newton's First Law.›

An object stays at rest, or keeps moving in a straight line at constant speed, unless a net external force acts on it.

First Law in symbols?›

F_net = 0 ⇔ a = 0 (v constant, including v = 0).

What is inertia?›

The tendency of an object to resist any change in its motion.

What measures inertia?›

Mass. More mass means it's harder to start, stop or turn.

What did Aristotle believe about motion?›

That a force is needed to keep an object moving, and rest is the natural state.

What did Galileo's inclined-plane thought experiment show?›

With no friction, a ball rolling onto a flat surface would roll on forever. Force is needed to change motion, not to keep it.

A jet cruises at constant velocity with 80 000 N thrust. How big is the air resistance?›

80 000 N. Constant velocity means F_net = 0.

Why do passengers lurch forward when a bus brakes?›

Their bodies keep moving forward (inertia) until the seat or belt pushes them back.

A stone whirled on a string is released. Which way does it go?›

Along the tangent, in a straight line. With no string force, nothing changes its velocity.

What is an inertial frame?›

A frame of reference that isn't accelerating. The First Law holds in it. beyond the test

Second Law: F = ma 12 cards
State Newton's Second Law.›

Acceleration is directly proportional to net force, inversely proportional to mass, and in the direction of the net force: F_net = m·a.

The two facts behind F = ma?›

a ∝ F_net (mass fixed) and a ∝ 1/m (force fixed). Combined: a ∝ F_net/m, and the newton is defined so the constant is 1.

Double the net force on the same mass: what happens to a?›

a doubles.

Double the mass with the same net force: what happens to a?›

a halves.

Do F_net and a always point the same way?›

Yes. Velocity can point elsewhere (a braking car: v forward, a backward).

How is the First Law a special case of the Second?›

Put F_net = 0 into a = F_net/m: a = 0, so v is constant.

30 000 kg jet, a = 1.5 m/s². Thrust?›

F = ma = 30 000 × 1.5 = 45 000 N

2000 N on a 1000 kg car. a?›

a = F/m = 2.0 m/s². Towing an identical car: 1.0 m/s².

A 1200 kg car goes 0 → 27 m/s in 9.0 s. Net force?›

a = 27/9.0 = 3.0 m/s², so F = 1200 × 3.0 = 3600 N.

Second Law in 2D?›

Separately on each axis: F_net,x = m·a_x and F_net,y = m·a_y.

Net force needed for circular motion?›

F_net = m·v²/r, toward the center. It is supplied by real forces (tension, friction, gravity).

Newton's own form of the Second Law?›

F_net = Δp/Δt, where p = mv is momentum. If m is constant this gives F = ma. beyond the test

Mass, weight & apparent weight 11 cards
Mass vs weight?›

Mass (kg) = amount of matter/inertia, the same everywhere. Weight (N) = the gravitational force on it, which depends on g.

Derive W = mg.›

In free fall the only force is W and a = g. N2: F_net = ma, so W = mg.

Weight of a 1 kg mass on Earth?›

9.81 N (about 2.2 lb).

A 60 kg student on the Moon (g = 1.62 m/s²): mass and weight?›

Mass 60 kg; weight 60 × 1.62 = 97 N.

Why do heavy and light objects fall with the same acceleration?›

a = W/m = mg/m = g. More weight, but also proportionally more mass (inertia).

What does a bathroom scale actually read?›

The normal force N it pushes up on you with, not your weight.

Scale reading in an accelerating elevator (up +)?›

N = m(g + a)

60 kg person, elevator accelerating up at 2.0 m/s². Scale?›

60 × 11.81 = 709 N (feels heavier).

Elevator moving DOWN but slowing down. Scale more or less than mg?›

More. Slowing while moving down means a points up.

Scale reading in free fall?›

0: "weightless". The weight is still mg; there's just no support force.

What is terminal velocity?›

The constant speed reached when air resistance grows to equal the weight: F_net = 0, so a = 0.

Third Law 11 cards
State Newton's Third Law.›

When A exerts a force on B, B exerts a force on A that is equal in size and opposite in direction: F(A on B) = −F(B on A).

Recipe for finding a reaction force?›

Write the action as "A exerts a force on B". The reaction is "B exerts a force on A". Swap A and B.

Four properties of a third-law pair?›

Same size · opposite direction · same type of force · act on different objects.

Why don't action and reaction cancel?›

They act on different objects. Forces cancel only when they act on the same object.

Earth pulls a ball down. Reaction?›

The ball pulls Earth up with the same force.

A book rests on a table. Are N and W a third-law pair?›

No. Both act on the book and are different types. They're equal because of the First Law (a = 0).

Reaction to "the table pushes up on the book"?›

The book pushes down on the table.

A tire pushes backward on the road. Reaction?›

The road pushes the tire forward, and that is what drives the car.

A bug hits a bus windshield. Which feels the bigger force? Which accelerates more?›

The same force. The bug accelerates far more, because a = F/m.

How does a horse pull a cart forward if the cart pulls back equally?›

Those two forces act on different objects. The horse pushes back on the ground and the ground pushes the horse forward.

Why can't you move a stalled car by pushing its dashboard from inside?›

That's an internal force. Only outside forces accelerate a system.

Free-body diagrams 8 cards
What is a free-body diagram?›

A sketch of one object showing every force acting on it as an arrow from the object.

Direction of the normal force?›

Perpendicular to the surface, pushing away from it.

Direction of tension?›

Along the rope, pulling away from the object.

Direction of friction?›

Parallel to the surface, opposing sliding (or the tendency to slide).

Forces on a puck sliding on frictionless ice?›

Only weight (down) and normal (up). Nothing pushes it forward.

Forces on a ball thrown upward, after it leaves your hand (no air)?›

Only weight, down. Its net force is down even while it's rising.

10 kg box; someone pushes down on it with 20 N. N?›

N = 98.1 + 20 = 118 N. N isn't always mg.

Tension in each of two vertical cords holding a 12 kg lamp?›

T = mg/2 = 58.9 N.

Friction 7 cards
Static vs kinetic friction?›

Static: surfaces not sliding, f_s adjusts up to μ_s·N. Kinetic: surfaces sliding, f_k = μ_k·N (constant).

Usually, which is bigger: μ_s or μ_k?›

μ_s. That's why it's harder to start a box moving than to keep it moving.

A 20 kg crate, μ_s = 0.45. A 60 N push doesn't move it. Friction?›

60 N. Static friction matches the push; 88 N is only the maximum.

Units of μ?›

None. It's a ratio of two forces (f/N).

Which friction pushes a car or walker forward?›

Static friction from the ground (the tire or foot isn't sliding).

Push needed to keep a crate sliding at constant velocity?›

Equal to kinetic friction: F = μ_k·N (F_net = 0).

A puck slows at 1.2 m/s². μ_k?›

μ_k = a/g = 1.2/9.81 ≈ 0.12. The mass cancels.

Inclines & connected objects 10 cards
Components of weight on an incline of angle θ?›

mg sinθ down the slope; mg cosθ into the slope.

Normal force on an incline?›

N = mg cosθ (if no other perpendicular forces).

Acceleration down a frictionless incline?›

a = g sinθ. It doesn't depend on mass.

Acceleration sliding down with kinetic friction?›

a = g(sinθ − μ_k cosθ)

What is the angle of repose?›

The steepest angle at which a block can rest: tanθ = μ_s.

Two masses joined by a string over a pulley: what's the same for both?›

The size of the acceleration and the tension.

Atwood machine acceleration?›

a = (m₂ − m₁)g/(m₁ + m₂)

Atwood machine tension?›

T = 2m₁m₂g/(m₁ + m₂), between m₁g and m₂g.

Block on a table pulled by a hanging mass: acceleration?›

a = (m₂ − μ_k m₁)g/(m₁ + m₂)

Is the string tension equal to the hanging weight m₂g?›

No, less, while m₂ accelerates down: T = m₂(g − a).
