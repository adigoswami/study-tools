Rules used by the checker
  1. Count: add the valence electrons (H 1, C 4, N 5, O 6, S 6, Cl 7). Negative ion: add 1 per − charge. Positive ion: subtract 1 per + charge.
  2. Skeleton: the central atom is the least electronegative one (it usually forms the most bonds). H and F are never central.
  3. Lone pairs: complete the outer atoms first; leftover electrons go on the central atom.
  4. Octets: if the central atom is short, move a lone pair from an outer atom into a double or triple bond. Every atom needs 8 electrons; H needs 2.
  5. Best structure: formal charge = valence − lone-pair electrons − number of bonds. The best structure keeps formal charges as close to 0 as possible. For nitrate, carbonate and nitrite, any resonance form is accepted.
Follows the 4-step method in the class Gen Chem Lewis slides and NCERT Class 11 Ch 4 (§4.1.3 Lewis structures, §4.1.4 formal charge). Sulfate is checked in its octet form.
