Valence electrons & dot symbols 10 cards
What are valence electrons?›

The electrons in an atom's outermost shell. They are the only electrons that take part in bonding.

How do you find the number of valence electrons for a main-group element?›

Use the last digit of its group number: group 1 → 1, group 2 → 2, groups 13–18 → 3 to 8.

Valence electrons of H, C, N, O?›

H 1 · C 4 · N 5 · O 6

Valence electrons of F, Cl, S, P?›

F 7 · Cl 7 · S 6 · P 5

What is a Lewis dot symbol?›

The element symbol with its valence electrons drawn as dots around it. Place one dot on each of the 4 sides first, then pair them up.

State the octet rule.›

Atoms bond so that each ends up with 8 electrons around it, like a noble gas.

How many electrons does hydrogen need? What is this called?›

2, called a duet (like helium).

How many bonds does C usually form? N? O? a halogen (F, Cl)?›

C 4 · N 3 · O 2 · halogen 1. That is the number of electrons each is missing from an octet.

In a neutral molecule, what does N usually have around it?›

3 bonds + 1 lone pair.

In a neutral molecule, what does O usually have around it?›

2 bonds + 2 lone pairs.

Bonds, bond order & bond length 11 cards
What is a Lewis structure?›

A drawing of a molecule or ion that shows how the atoms share valence electrons so that each gets an octet (a duet for H).

What is a bonding pair?›

A shared pair of electrons between two atoms. It counts toward the octet of both atoms.

What is a lone pair?›

A pair of electrons that belongs to only one atom and is not involved in bonding.

How many electrons does one dash (–) represent?›

2 electrons (one shared pair).

How many electrons are shared in a double bond? A triple bond?›

Double: 4 (2 pairs). Triple: 6 (3 pairs).

What is bond order?›

The number of bonds between two atoms: single = 1, double = 2, triple = 3.

As bond order increases, what happens to bond length and bond strength?›

Length gets shorter; strength (the energy needed to break it) gets higher.

Rank C–C, C=C, C≡C from shortest to longest.›

C≡C (1.203 Å) < C=C (1.339 Å) < C–C (1.535 Å).

Rank C–C, C=C, C≡C from weakest to strongest.›

C–C (365 kJ/mol) < C=C (598 kJ/mol) < C≡C (962 kJ/mol).

What is the bond order in N₂? In O₂? In F₂?›

N≡N: 3 · O=O: 2 · F–F: 1.

Which is shorter: the C–O bond in CH₃OH or the C=O bond in CH₂O?›

The C=O bond. A higher bond order means a shorter bond.

The 4-step method 10 cards
Step 1 of drawing a Lewis structure?›

Count the total valence electrons of all the atoms (adjusted for the charge if it's an ion).

Step 2?›

Connect the atoms with single bonds to make the skeleton, with the central atom in the middle.

Step 3?›

Add lone pairs to the outer atoms to complete their octets. Put any leftover electrons on the central atom.

Step 4?›

If the central atom still lacks an octet, move lone pairs from outer atoms into double or triple bonds.

How do you choose the central atom?›

The least electronegative atom, which usually forms the most bonds.

Which two atoms can never be the central atom? Why?›

H and F. Each can form only one bond.

Does step 4 add new electrons?›

No. It only moves an existing lone pair so that it becomes shared.

What two checks should you do at the end?›

(1) Electrons drawn = electrons counted. (2) Every atom has 8 electrons around it (H: 2).

Why does CO₂ need double bonds?›

After step 3, both O atoms are full but C has only 4 electrons. Moving one lone pair from each O gives O=C=O.

Why does HCN need a triple bond?›

After step 3, C has only 4 electrons. Moving two lone pairs from N gives C≡N, so C and N both have 8.

Polyatomic ions 18 cards
How does a charge change step 1 for an ion?›

A negative charge: add 1 electron per − charge. A positive charge: subtract 1 per + charge.

How do you show the charge on a Lewis structure of an ion?›

Put square brackets around the whole structure and write the charge outside, top right.

What holds the atoms inside a polyatomic ion together?›

Covalent bonds (shared pairs). The whole group carries one overall charge.

Total valence electrons in OH⁻?›

6 + 1 + 1 = 8

Total valence electrons in NH₄⁺?›

5 + 4 − 1 = 8

Total valence electrons in H₃O⁺?›

6 + 3 − 1 = 8

Total valence electrons in CN⁻?›

4 + 5 + 1 = 10

Total valence electrons in NO₃⁻?›

5 + 18 + 1 = 24

Total valence electrons in CO₃²⁻?›

4 + 18 + 2 = 24

Total valence electrons in SO₄²⁻?›

6 + 24 + 2 = 32

Total valence electrons in PO₄³⁻?›

5 + 24 + 3 = 32

Total valence electrons in NO₂⁻?›

5 + 12 + 1 = 18

Describe the Lewis structure of OH⁻.›

[O–H]⁻ with 3 lone pairs on O.

Describe the Lewis structure of NH₄⁺.›

[N with 4 single bonds to H]⁺, and no lone pairs.

Describe the Lewis structure of CN⁻.›

[:C≡N:]⁻, a triple bond with one lone pair on each atom.

Describe the Lewis structure of NO₃⁻.›

N in the centre; one N=O (O has 2 lone pairs) and two N–O (each O has 3 lone pairs); brackets, − charge.

Describe the Lewis structure of CO₃²⁻.›

C in the centre; one C=O and two C–O. Same pattern as nitrate, with a 2− charge.

Describe the octet form of SO₄²⁻.›

S in the centre with 4 single bonds to O; each O has 3 lone pairs; S has none; brackets, 2− charge.

Formal charge & resonance 12 cards
Formula for formal charge?›

FC = V − L − ½B, where V = valence electrons of the free atom, L = electrons in its lone pairs, and B = electrons in its bonds. So ½B is simply the number of bonds.

What must the formal charges in a structure add up to?›

The overall charge of the molecule or ion (0 for a neutral molecule).

How does formal charge help you pick the best structure?›

The best structure has formal charges closest to zero, with any negative charge on the more electronegative atom.

Formal charge on O in OH⁻?›

6 − 6 − 1 = −1

Formal charge on N in NH₄⁺?›

5 − 0 − 4 = +1

Formal charges in NO₃⁻?›

N +1; O in N=O 0; each O in N–O −1. Sum = −1 ✓

Formal charges in the octet form of SO₄²⁻?›

S +2; each O −1. Sum = +2 − 4 = −2 ✓

Why is O=C=O better than O≡C–O?›

Both give octets, but O=C=O has all formal charges 0; O≡C–O has +1 and −1.

What are resonance structures?›

Two or more valid Lewis structures that differ only in where the electrons (double bonds) are placed. They are joined with ↔.

How many resonance structures do NO₃⁻ and CO₃²⁻ each have?›

3. The double bond can go to any of the 3 O atoms.

What does resonance tell us about the real nitrate ion?›

It is a blend: all three N–O bonds are identical, somewhere between a single and a double bond (bond order 1⅓).

How many resonance structures does NO₂⁻ have?›

2

Exceptions to the octet rule 4 cards
Name the three types of exception to the octet rule.›

Incomplete octet · odd number of electrons · expanded octet.

Give an example of an incomplete octet.›

BF₃: B has only 6 electrons. BeCl₂: Be has only 4.

Give an example of an odd-electron molecule.›

NO or NO₂. The total is odd, so one atom must have 7 electrons.

Which atoms can have an expanded octet? Give an example.›

Atoms in period 3 or below (P, S, Cl…), e.g. PCl₅ (10 e⁻ on P) and SF₆ (12 e⁻ on S). beyond NCERT
