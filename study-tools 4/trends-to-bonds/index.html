Reactivity series of metals

← most reactive · lowest ionization enthalpyleast reactive →

Why is K at the top? Down group 1 the atoms get bigger (Li 152 → Na 186 → K 231 pm), so the single outer electron sits farther from the nucleus and is held less tightly. First ionization enthalpy decreases down the group, so the electron comes off more easily and the metal is more reactive: K is above Na.

Across period 3 it runs the other way. Na (496 kJ/mol) loses its electron more easily than Mg (737 kJ/mol), so Na sits above Mg. Metals on the left and lower down lose electrons most easily, and that is the job a metal does in an ionic bond.

First ionization enthalpy, period 3

The energy needed to pull off the outermost electron rises across the period (Na 496 → Si 786). Al dips below Mg, which is a known exception: Al's outer electron is in a higher-energy p orbital. Al still has to lose three electrons to become Al3+, so it sits below Mg in the series.

    The rules

    • First element: named as the element. Mono- is left off the first element: CO2 is carbon dioxide, not “monocarbon dioxide”. It gets a prefix only when there is more than one atom (N2O4: dinitrogen).
    • Second element: takes a prefix (mono- included) and the -ide ending: CO is carbon monoxide.
    • Dropped vowel: the final a or o of the prefix is dropped before “oxide”: monoxide, tetroxide, pentoxide (not monooxide, tetraoxide, pentaoxide).
    • Hydrogen first: no prefix goes before hydrogen, whatever the count: H2S is hydrogen sulfide, not dihydrogen sulfide.
    • Common names: some compounds are known only by common names: H2O is water, NH3 is ammonia.
