Show answer

Answer: C) Valid Lewis structures that differ only in where the electrons (double bonds) are placed. The atoms stay in the same places; only the position of the double bond (electrons) changes. They are joined by ↔.

31Polyatomic Ions · free response

Draw the Lewis structure of the sulfite ion, SO₃²⁻. Show all 4 steps, and give the formal charge on every atom.

Show worked solution
  • Step 1: 6 + 3(6) + 2 = 26 valence electrons.
  • Step 2: S is central (less electronegative than O). Draw 3 S–O single bonds: 6 electrons used, 20 left. Draw brackets with 2− outside.
  • Step 3: each O gets 3 lone pairs (18 electrons), leaving 2. Those go on S as 1 lone pair.
  • Step 4: S has 3 bonds + 1 lone pair = 8, and each O has 8. Nothing to move.
  • Formal charges: S = 6 − 2 − 3 = +1; each O = 6 − 6 − 1 = −1. Sum = +1 − 3 = −2, matching the charge.
32Formal Charge & Resonance · free response

Draw ALL the resonance structures of the carbonate ion, CO₃²⁻, and explain what the real ion looks like.

Show worked solution
  • Step 1: 4 + 3(6) + 2 = 24 valence electrons.
  • Steps 2–3: C in the centre with 3 single bonds (6 e⁻), then 3 lone pairs on each O (18 e⁻), so 0 left. C has only 6 electrons.
  • Step 4: move one lone pair from one O into a C=O double bond. C now has 8. That O has 2 lone pairs; the other two have 3 each (formal charge −1 each).
  • The double bond can go to any of the 3 O atoms, giving 3 resonance structures joined by ↔, all in brackets with 2−.
  • The real ion is a blend: all three C–O bonds are identical, between a single and a double bond (bond order 1⅓).
