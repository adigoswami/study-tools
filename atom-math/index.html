Isotopes, Ions and Average Atomic Mass

How to use: pick a mode, answer, then press Check. Keep a periodic table and a calculator next to you.

Count particles

Quick rules
  • Protons = atomic number (the bottom-left number). Protons tell you the element.
  • Neutrons = mass number − atomic number.
  • Electrons = protons − charge. In a neutral atom, electrons = protons.
  • A positive ion (cation) has lost electrons. A negative ion (anion) has gained electrons.
Isotope or ion?

Quick rules
  • Isotopes: same protons, different neutrons, so different mass numbers.
  • Ions: same protons, different electrons.
  • Different protons always means a different element.
  • A positive ion keeps the element name: sodium ion. A negative ion ends in -ide: chloride ion.
  • Hyphen notation: element name-mass number, like carbon-14.

    Quick rules
    • Average atomic mass = sum of (isotope mass × fraction). Fraction = percent ÷ 100. Unit: amu.
    • The percents add to 100, so the fractions add to 1.
    • Two isotopes with unknown percents: let the fractions be x and (1 − x).
    • It is a weighted average, so it sits closest to the mass of the most abundant isotope.
    • Answers are accepted within 0.02 amu or 0.3 percentage points.
